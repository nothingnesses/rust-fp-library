# Cross-Check Against the Prior Reviews

The main review-2 documents were written without consulting the earlier review material. Afterwards, `docs/plans/effects/review/` (four staged reviews, 2026-05) and `docs/plans/effects/review-1/` (findings, remediation plan, and W-workstream spike documents) were restored and read in full, and every claim relevant to the current code was fact-checked against the snapshot at commit `2e0a7417`. This document records: which prior findings have been resolved (so they are not re-imported as open issues), which prior points are correct, still relevant, and missing or under-weighted in review-2, where review-2 disagrees with the priors, and which review-2 findings are net-new.

A note on the W-labels: the `W1`/`W11`/`W13` markers found in source docs (see organisation section 4) are pointers into `review-1/remediation-plan.md`'s workstreams. That plan is the missing context for several things review-2 observed in the code.

## 1. Prior findings that are resolved (do not re-import)

Verified against the current code:

- `expand`/`weaken` missing (review-1 P0): implemented since (W1), as the structural traversal review-2 describes. The W1 spike (`review-1/w1-row-embed-spike.md`) records why a coercion-based or `NaturalTransformation`-based implementation was rejected (per-call embedding evidence cannot be carried by the fixed trait method signature; boundary frames must not be lowered through the public Free view). This is precisely the documentation review-2's R10 asks to surface in `run.md`; the content already exists in the spike and can be lifted rather than rewritten.
- No code generation for the wrapper x effect cross-product (review-1 P0): implemented since (W2) as the registry-driven `define_effect!`/`define_run_wrapper!` that review-2 describes. Review-2's organisation findings about where that codegen lives are about the W2 implementation, not about its absence.
- Subsystem not feature-gated (review-1 P1): implemented since (W4/W4a); the `effects` feature exists, default-off, with macro diagnostics and the `effects-feature-off` recipe.
- Effect catalog limited to State/Reader/Except/Writer/Choose/Empty (review/3): the W11/W12 ports landed Fresh, Input, Output, KVStore, Log, Fail, and Coroutine with their runners, exactly as recommended.
- No async interpreter; `spawn_blocking` is the only story (review-1 section 6, "the single biggest functional gap"): superseded by the W13 milestone (`Await` base-lift effect plus `Run::run_async`). Review-2's finding that `interpreter.rs` still carries the old "no async ships" text is the residue of this transition; the W13 resume notes in `review-1/remediation-plan.md` confirm the shipped surface.
- Standard scoped values misnamed as dispatchers, incomplete re-exports, stale module docs (review/2 findings 1, 2, and structural issues 1 to 3): the renames and export fixes happened (`standard_scoped_handlers`, handler vocabulary, `Dispatch*` reserved for protocols).
- Writer "only half complete" and NonDet "incomplete without Empty" (review/2 findings 5, 6): both completed (censor pre/post, listen, fold_writer/run_writer; Empty plus run_nondet/run_first_success).
- The two adversarial reviews (`review/0_first_order_effects_implementation`, `review/1_scoped_effects_design`) target much earlier designs; their fundamental flaws (a `run_accum` that ignored its init argument, structurally reserved but panicking scoped effects, unspecified scoped-handler interception) are all resolved or superseded by the boundary/carrier design. Two of their observations survive in current form and are listed in section 2.

## 2. Correct, still-relevant prior points that review-2 missed or under-weighted

These are fact-checked as still true at `2e0a7417` and should be read as additions to the review-2 documents:

1. Generic scoped rows are unsupported (review-1 section 9; W9, deferred). `define_scoped_row!` rejects type parameters with "generic scoped rows are deferred", so reusable environment/error-parameterized scoped stacks cannot be defined as row markers. Review-2 did not flag this. W9's wait-for-a-concrete-need stance is reasonable; the limitation belongs in `run.md`'s limits section either way.
2. The scoped-dispatch consolidation question was already audited, with numbers and a verdict (W8, `review-1/w8-consolidation-feasibility.md`). The audit measured the per-wrapper plumbing at roughly 83 percent mechanical (about 203 of 1,200 lines differ between the Rc and Arc raw-scoped modules after normalization, consistent with review-2's independent diff measurements), found the per-effect semantic handlers bespoke and non-generatable, and decided: preserve the shipped design, reject full consolidation, defer partial descriptor-backed generation behind concrete triggers (adding a new scoped effect, adding a seventh wrapper, or recurring maintenance burden across the Box/Rc/Arc plumbing). Review-2's R2 must be read together with this verdict. The honest synthesis: R2 re-raises what W8 deferred, and the trigger condition is not hypothetical, because review-2's own port candidates (streaming, ChooseH, CC/Shift) would add scoped effects and therefore fire the W8 trigger. When that happens, W8's prescription (generate the mechanical plumbing only, prove equivalence via the same cargo-expand discipline W2 used, keep semantic handlers hand-written) is the right execution plan, and R2 should adopt it rather than propose a fresh approach. Review/2's structural issue 5 ("focus on private helpers and shared tests, not premature generic abstraction") is the same caution and stands.
3. The runtime policy review-2 asked for already exists in part (W13). Review-2's coverage candidate 9 proposes "a decision document on the deferred runtime-sensitive set". `review-1/remediation-plan.md` records an adopted W13 runtime policy with its research base (`w13-runtime-research.md`, seven libraries plus a switch-resume probe) and spike evidence (`w13-async-spike.md`). The candidate should be narrowed to: extend the adopted W13 policy to cover the still-open items it explicitly lists, namely the Rc/Arc async design choice (single-shot futures versus cloneable `Shared` futures), async-aware scoped dispatch, and the criteria for Unlift/Provider/Parallel/Timer/Subprocess. The switch-resume idea review-2's external-ideas file mentions for CC/Shift was already absorbed into the async driver design ("keep the continuation as data"), which strengthens rather than changes that suggestion.
4. Shift/CC is answer-type-polymorphic continuation capture, not merely multi-shot resumption (review-1 section 10; review/3 defer table). Review-2's coverage candidate 8 says the Rc/Arc families are "structurally reachable" for Shift-style effects; that is true for continuation capture, but the priors correctly identify the harder part: the captured continuation's answer type must be managed polymorphically, which the mono-in-A model does not give for free. The candidate's "needs its own design round" stands, with this sharper statement of why.
5. The Fn versus FnOnce asymmetry is a user-facing ergonomic edge (review-1 section 9; review/0 M7). Wrapper `bind` takes `FnOnce` closures while handler closures must be `Fn` (callable through `&self` dispatch inside `tail_rec_m`), so moving captured state between the two requires interior mutability or refcounted captures. Documented in `first_order.rs` and `run.rs` docs, still true, and absent from review-2's documents; it belongs in the user-facing guide alongside the captured-cell idiom.
6. File sizes are doctest-dominated (review-1 section 7). Review-2's line-count figures (35k lines across wrapper families) are accurate but include extensive doctests; the prior review's nuance ("the cost is the six-fold parallelism, not the per-file length") is the correct reading of those numbers and review-2 adopts it.
7. The brand and class capability matrix history (review-1 section 7; W3). The uneven Brand-level class coverage review-2 summarizes from the `types/effects.rs` table is the implemented W3 capability audit; what remains open from W3 is the decide-the-gaps half (whether to brand the erased family at all, whether the Explicit trio's missing owned/Send class impls stay documented gaps or get closed). Review-2's architecture question "do all six wrappers earn their keep" is the natural sequel to that open half.
8. A brand-prefix legend is still missing (review-1 section 8). The priors asked for a single table mapping prefix to closure storage to wrappers; no such legend exists in `run.md` today (review-2's organisation table 2.1 is that legend and can be lifted into the docs).
9. Missing-handler diagnostics remain raw trait errors (review/2 findings 4 and 8; W7). The W7 hardening added the reading guide review-2 found in `handlers.rs`; the deeper improvement (error anchors that name the missing brand) was explored and parked. Review-2's R1 brand-keyed dispatch would change those errors to "no handler for brand X" as a side effect, which is worth counting among its benefits.
10. Row-witness spelling noise (review/2 finding 3 and issue 7). Still current: standard handler constructors need explicit row-minus aliases (`local_handler::<_, ReaderRemoved, _>()`), and tests define many `FirstRowMinusX` aliases. Review-2 touches this only via R1 and the `run.md` row-minus note; the priors' framing (try inference improvements first, then a narrow row-alias helper, avoid a broad effect-stack macro until proven necessary) is a useful constraint on R1's design.

## 3. Disagreements between review-2 and the priors

Recorded so the contradiction is explicit rather than discovered later:

- Naming of the brand siblings. Review-1 section 8 calls the `Box`/`Send`/bare prefix scheme "coherent"; review-2 organisation 2.1 argues it is internally inconsistent (prefix names a pointer in one case and a bound in another, the bare name belongs to the non-default family, the Box flavour carries a redundant `P`). The review-2 position is the stronger one under the project's breaking-changes-acceptable principle, but the priors' point that the scheme is at least documented per brand is fair; the disagreement is about whether documentation suffices.
- `named_helpers` placement. Review-1 section 7 calls the split "a good call" (keeps wrapper files small); review-2 organisation 1.1 recommends folding helpers into per-effect modules so each effect has one home per crate. Both optimize different axes (file size versus locality); this is a judgment call to make once, ideally as part of R2/W8-trigger work when files get regenerated anyway.
- `ScopedCoproduct`/`ScopedNil`. Review-1 praises the aliases; review-2 notes they enforce nothing and must not be mistaken for a safety boundary. Not a real conflict (both say they are documentation), but the emphasis differs.
